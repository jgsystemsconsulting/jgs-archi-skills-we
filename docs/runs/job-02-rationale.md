## Purpose
Agree the sales-to-mill capability set that runs promise, order, mill schedule, and collect at Hatherley Works, with stable names for later views.

## Stakeholders and Concerns
Plant Manager and Head of Sales sign the map. MES Owner and CRM Programme Manager hold the MillOS vs OrderSight boundary in documentation. Operations Planner needs the promise-to-schedule capability chain named. Integration Lead is omitted from this view; IDs remain from job 1.

## Viewpoint
Capability Map viewpoint (overview). View name Capability Map.

## Questions Answered
Which top-level capabilities cover promise, order, mill schedule, and collect? Who owns mill schedule capability language (MillOS) versus sales-facing promise/order (OrderSight)?

## Assumptions
Named systems appear only in documentation. No processes, applications, nodes, or work packages belong on this view. Job 1 motivation is reused, not redrawn.

## Decisions
Four plain top-level capabilities: Promise, Order, Mill schedule, Collect. Flow chain left to right. MillOS owns mill schedule capability in docs; OrderSight stays sales-facing; no ERP replacement capability.

## Exclusions
Motivation redraw, business processes, applications, technology/physical nodes, work packages, second site, costing, MES rewrite, WMS/TMS/data lake/ERP replacement. Integration Lead not placed on this view.

## Open Questions
None for this overview grain. Later SOAM jobs add process, application, and technology layers.
